\title{GwenLaya v4: a pre-registered study of answering only when a check can back the answer\\[4pt]
\large Interim report: registered design, deviations, a claim audit, exploratory\\
full-E baselines on TPU, a math gate, a 9B tier, and a trained Laya tested on a fresh math and Python set\\ with three deviations and a failed control, and then on a fresh Rust set where its advantage did not appear}
\author{Xavier Callens\\AutoevolveAI / ANSE open-source project}
\date{10 October 2026 \quad (revision 3.9.0, branch \texttt{feat/py-depth}; supersedes 3.8.0, 3.7.1, 3.7.0 and 3.6.0)}

\begin{abstract}
GwenLaya is a system design in which a small open model (Qwen3.5 family) answers a programming or
mathematics question only when an executed check or a calibrated confidence score supports the
answer, and otherwise abstains or escalates to a larger tier. We pre-registered fifteen hypotheses
(two primary: fewer confident-wrong answers than log-probability selective prediction at matched
coverage, H1; lower cost per correct answered item than the largest tier with the gate, H3; \textbf{as tested on E$'$, H1 is the difference in area under the risk-coverage curve against raw log-prob and H3 is the difference in selective risk against the gate alone at the gate's coverage, not the registered cost-per-correct metric}) before
any GwenLaya evaluation data were generated. \textbf{Revision 3.6.0 added a deviated test of the two
primaries on a fresh math and Python evaluation set E$'$ (Section~\ref{sec:laya}): on that set H1 is supported and H3 is not. Revision 3.7.0 ran the same frozen Laya on a
fresh Rust set R$'$ (Section~\ref{sec:rfresh}): neither primary is supported there, so the E$'$ result is a math and Python result and has not been shown to carry over to Rust.}
It carries deviations D48 (split), D49 (Laya is trained on E) and D50 (the registered shuffled-label control failed
and was withdrawn; applied as written, E$'$ would not have been generated). No other registered hypothesis has been tested, and no test
ran in the registered form.

\textbf{Revision 3.6.0.} Laya here is Answer.AI's \code{ModernBERT-large} with a rank-16 LoRA adapter and a router and a calibrator head,
trained on a TPU v5e (torch\_xla) on 3,081 tasks (E, 1,536, plus 1,545 from a fresh pool P) with labels from Qwen3.5-2B, 4B and 9B. The data-lake
``laya'' weights were not used. A calibration split C (283 tasks) fixes an isotonic map; the artifacts were frozen in commit
\code{d217f1e} before E$'$ (828 tasks: 659 math, 169 Python) was generated. On E$'$, with the 9B as primary tier, the calibrated Laya
P(correct) has a lower area under the risk-coverage curve than the raw mean log-prob (difference $-0.078$, 95\% CI $-0.106$ to $-0.053$; Holm $p=0.0002$ over H1 and H3), and the same holds for the 2B and 4B (H1). Against the executed gate alone, at
the gate's coverage, the difference in selective risk is $-0.012$ (CI $-0.023$ to $+0.011$), and H3 is not supported on any tier. Exploratory:
a logistic regression on hand-built features (gate, mean and min log-prob, domain, tier) cannot be told apart from Laya on AURC (the LR's point estimate is slightly better), and
Laya's AUROC advantage over it is $0.013$ (CI $0.001$ to $0.026$) on the 9B; the gain over the raw log-prob and the gate alone may come largely from combining them (a reading; the text-only ablation of 3.8.0, Section~\ref{sec:textonly}, removes the numeric inputs, not the text, so it does not settle this).
\textbf{The shuffled-label controls failed the registered 0.4 to 0.6 criterion on C} (calibrator 0.693; router 0.572 / 0.637 / 0.389), the untrained twin scored 0.480 pooled and 0.239 to 0.514 within strata, and the criterion was withdrawn (D50) and replaced by a leakage check (E$'$: 0 overlap by cluster, task id and prompt hash); our reading of the failure is not proven.
An exploratory router (threshold chosen on C) reaches accuracy 0.705 against 0.696 for always-9B, with a confident-wrong rate of 4.0\% against 30.4\% (the policies answer different sets of tasks) and a cost of
0.79 of always-9B's (0.89 with each run's first compile chunk zeroed). Qwen3.5-27B did not load in 33 minutes on a four-chip slice, a generator LoRA probe finished no step in 25 minutes, and a first Laya run was lost (D52).
E$'$ has 169 Python tasks and mostly easy math, and no Rust.

\textbf{Revision 3.8.0.} One exploratory ablation (A16, one seed, descriptive, no hypothesis tested): the same Laya calibrator retrained with only the domain, tier, prompt and candidate text (no gate or log-prob input). On E$'$ it reaches AUROC 0.839, 0.869 and 0.870 (2B, 4B, 9B; CIs above 0.5), above the raw log-prob and below the hand-feature regression, with the full Laya above it by about 0.08; on the fresh Rust set R$'$ it is at chance (AUROC 0.405, 0.555, 0.588; CIs include 0.5). Section~\ref{sec:textonly} gives the numbers and the hypotheses we did not test.

\textbf{Revision 3.9.0.} Four additions; none is a hypothesis test, and none required new generations or GCP spend. (1) A17, the Python visible-test depth dial on the stored E answers (524 tasks; Section~\ref{sec:a17}): giving the gate the first $k=1,2,4,8$ hidden test cases instead of one lowers the confident-wrong rate from 0.139 [0.111, 0.170] to 0.036 [0.021, 0.053] (4B) and from 0.147 [0.116, 0.177] to 0.046 [0.029, 0.065] (9B), while gate coverage falls from 0.815 to 0.712 and from 0.872 to 0.771; the pattern is that of the Rust dial of 3.7.0. The A17 plan said these answers came from prompts that showed no tests. \textbf{That is false}: every MBPP prompt (373 of the 529 Python tasks) prints the gate's assert verbatim, and 71 of the 156 HumanEval prompts carry doctest examples (D63). (2) \textbf{A corrected headline.} The 4B math gate's confident-wrong rate of 0.026 was set against 0.388 ``when everything is answered''; 0.388 counts the 160 math tasks with no boxed answer (mostly generations cut at 1,024 tokens) as confident-wrong. Against answering whenever a boxed answer exists, the comparison is 0.026 against 0.068 (registered scorer). (3) The registered math scorer missed notational equivalences (D62). An exploratory, post-hoc re-score of the stored answers raises math accuracy from 0.514, 0.612 and 0.642 to 0.558, 0.664 and 0.692 (2B, 4B, 9B) and lowers the math gate's VERIFIED-but-wrong count from 7, 13 and 10 to 2, 0 and 0 of 500; under it the 4B and 9B math gates have no confident-wrong answer on E (rule-of-three 95\% upper bound 0.006), against 0.016 and 0.002 for answer-if-boxed. The registered numbers remain the primary record; the re-score was not propagated into A4, A5, A11, A12 or the Laya labels. (4) System changes, with no results: an opt-in weak-gate verdict policy (off by default; every VERIFIED of the stored E gate rests on one visible test), two Lean verdict rules, and a hardened Lean~4 gate (a trusted second Lean process compares the elaborated theorem type with the reference statement and audits axioms with \code{collectAxioms}) written after an internal audit found soundness holes in the earlier gate (Sections~\ref{sec:policy390}, \ref{sec:lean390}). A Lean miniF2F run (A18) is planned and pre-registered, with 223 of 244 statements passing the sanity check. Its generation started after the A18 amendment and is in progress (the sampling part is being re-run); \textbf{no Lean result is reported in this revision}.

\textbf{Revision 3.7.0.} R$'$ is built from Exercism's Rust track (MIT): of 106 practice exercises, 69 are kept because their reference solution is verified by the
real checker and the unmodified starter stub is not; R$'$ is used for evaluation only (addendum A14, committed before any model generated for it). The frozen Laya, isotonic map
included, was run on the answers of Qwen3.5-2B, 4B and 9B. Accuracy is 6/69, 28/69 and 37/69. The executed gate (first test only) verifies 21, 40 and 45 answers with precision
0.286, 0.700 and 0.822, and its confident-wrong rate is 0.217, 0.174 and 0.116 against 0.913, 0.594 and 0.464 when everything is answered. On R$'$ the 9B H1 difference
(Laya minus raw log-prob, AURC) is $-0.070$ (95\% CI $-0.180$ to $+0.041$; Holm $p=0.20$), not supported on any tier; with 69 tasks this is underpowered and is not evidence of no effect.
The H3 difference is exactly $0$ on every tier, because Laya's top-$k$ tasks at the gate's coverage are the gate-verified tasks themselves. Exploratory: Laya is below the hand-feature logistic regression
(AUROC difference $-0.039$ [$-0.117$, $+0.023$] on the 9B, $-0.067$ [$-0.144$, $-0.001$] on the 4B, $-0.153$ [$-0.258$, $-0.070$] on the 2B). The router with the old $\tau=0.5$ has the accuracy of always-9B
(0.536) and a confident-wrong rate of 0.116 against 0.464, but a cost ratio of 0.987 (CI 0.946 to 1.024), i.e.\ no saving, against 0.79 on E$'$. We also report a harness property found while building R$'$: the Rust oracle
compiled without \code{--edition} (edition 2015). Re-checking every stored Rust answer under edition 2021 (A15.1) changed at most one hidden-test verdict per tier, always fail to pass (E 4B 1/507, E 9B 1/507, R$'$ 4B 1/69); published numbers stay labelled edition 2015 and the oracle default is now 2021 (D58). Reasons for the non-transfer are hypotheses only.
\textbf{Improving the gate (Section~\ref{sec:gate-improve}).} Descriptively, on the stored Rust answers of E (505 tasks), showing the gate $k=1,2,3$ visible assertions instead of one lowers the confident-wrong rate from 0.067 to 0.024 (4B) and from 0.061 to 0.014 (9B) while gate coverage falls from 0.594 to 0.550 and from 0.648 to 0.600. This is a property of the stored answers, not a hypothesis test; it was not run on Python or on R$'$, and the frozen Laya was not changed.

Revision 3.4.0 added \emph{exploratory, unregistered} baselines on the full E set (1,536 tasks:
Python 529, Rust 507, math 500). Revision 3.5.0 adds three further exploratory analyses on the same
E set, all post hoc: (A10) the registered program-of-thought math gate, which 3.4.0 reported as not
implemented; (A11) cross-fitted calibrated abstention; (A12) a Qwen3.5-9B tier on a four-chip TPU
slice with gate cascades. Models: Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct, Qwen3.5-2B, 4B and 9B. All used
greedy decoding with one sample, in bf16 under vLLM on TPU v5e, and all outputs were scored locally
in a sandbox. E is burned for the registered H1/H3 (Section~\ref{sec:verdicts}); 3.6.0 therefore uses a fresh E$'$. The 3.4.0 analysis
plan was committed after generation and as the 2B/4B scoring started, before any scored outcome was
inspected but after smaller descriptive runs on 80 tasks had shown approximate pass rates. The A10,
A11 and A12 addenda were each written before their results were computed, but after the E baselines
had been seen. \textbf{This revision supersedes the 3.4.0 statement that the math gate has coverage
0}: that was an implementation gap in our checker, now closed (Section~\ref{sec:a10}).

Pooled pass rates on the hidden checks were 0.423 (2B), 0.606 (4B), 0.653 (9B) and 0.482 (Coder).
Most of the findings are negative or null:
(i) Raw $\exp(\text{mean log-prob})$ confidence is badly calibrated. On the 4B its mean is
0.927--0.938 per domain, while only 0.529--0.675 of answers pass.
(ii) A gate cascade (2B, then an executed gate, then the 4B if the gate does not verify) is
``GwenLaya without Laya'' and is not a registered arm. With the program-of-thought math gate and its
generation cost charged, it gains no accuracy over always using the 4B: the pooled difference is
$-0.001$ (CI $-0.013$ to $0.012$). Pooled, it costs about twice the accelerator time (cost ratio 2.021,
CI 1.933--2.111, with the first compilation chunk; 2.023, CI 1.912--2.134, without it), because on math
it pays for programs and usually for both tiers. On Python alone it breaks even with warm-up (0.997) and
is about 10\% cheaper without it (0.900), at a 2.6-point accuracy cost. Cost is configuration dependent
and these ratios are not robust.
(iii) The program-of-thought math gate (A10) verifies 0.454 of the 4B's math answers (CI 0.410--0.498) with
precision 0.943 (CI 0.911--0.971), just below the 0.95 flag point of the registration, and the CI includes it.
Its confident-wrong rate over all math tasks is 0.026. \textbf{Corrected in 3.9.0:} revisions 3.5.0 to 3.8.0 set this against 0.388 ``when everything is answered'', but 0.388 counts the 160 tasks without a boxed answer (160 of the 4B's 194 wrong rows; mostly truncated) as confident-wrong. Answering whenever a boxed answer exists gives 0.068 at coverage 0.680 with the registered scorer, and 0.016 under the exploratory re-score of D62, where the gate's own rate is 0 of 500 (Section~\ref{sec:a10}).
(iv) A tier gap does change the cascade result (A12). Using a fixed reading rule written before the 9B
results (a cascade pays off only if it is cheaper than always-9B in both the with- and without-warm-up
readings and its accuracy CI does not exclude zero on the harmful side), 4B$\to$9B pays off for
Python and Rust but not for math (programs are charged for each tier consulted) and not pooled
(cost ratio 1.381 with warm-up and 1.724 without, although pooled accuracy is higher by 0.037).
2B$\to$9B on Python fails on accuracy ($-0.042$, CI $-0.068$ to $-0.015$). The 9B's effective engine
concurrency was not measured, so its cost is less certain.
(v) A cross-fitted logistic score (A11) that includes the gate verdict ranks and calibrates better than
log-prob alone (4B pooled AUROC 0.931 against 0.708). Because it uses the gate as a feature, that is a
calibration gain, not independent evidence. Its ECE bootstrap intervals sometimes exclude their own point
estimates, a known bias of percentile bootstrap for ECE.

The most favourable observation remains the 4B gate. Answering only when the executed gate verifies
gave fewer confident-wrong answers than a log-prob threshold at the same coverage: $-0.076$ for
Python, $-0.112$ for Rust and, with the new math gate, $-0.084$ for math. The baseline answers the $k$
most confident tasks, with $k$ equal to the gate's answered count in the same domain. Several caveats

\section{Retraction and the v3 gate failure, stated first}\label{sec:retraction}

\paragraph{Retraction.} Revision 3.1.0 of the predecessor report~\cite{gwayav3} claimed that the
low-tier optimizer improved pass rates by at least $+8.0$ percentage points. That claim rested on a
hand-written results file with invented numbers (A0 33\,\%, A3 100\,\%). The file was fabricated, it
has been deleted, and it is not evidence (\code{results/PREREGISTRATION.md}). The claim is
retracted. Nothing in this paper depends on it.

\paragraph{The measured v3 gate failed.} The pre-registered v3 gate was
$\text{A3}-\text{A0}\ge +8.0$ points pass@1 on MBPP-sanitized with \code{qwen2.5-coder:1.5b}. Both
measured runs \textbf{FAIL} that gate:
\begin{itemize}
\item $n=60$, CPU according to the file name and README (the file itself records hardware
  ``unspecified''): A3$-$A0 $=+6.67$ points, 95\% CI $[1.67, 13.33]$, 4 gained / 0 lost, exact
  McNemar $p=0.125$; verdict FAIL (\code{results/results_n60_cpu.json}).
\item $n=257$, L4 GPU: A0 64.2\,\%, A3 71.98\,\%, A3$-$A0 $=+7.78$ points, 95\% CI $[4.67, 11.28]$,
  20 gained / 0 lost; verdict FAIL against $+8.0$. This file's \code{git_commit} field is empty
  (\code{results/results_n257_l4_gpu.json}).
\end{itemize}
At $n=60$ the difference is not significant by the exact McNemar test ($p=0.125$, 4/0
discordant pairs). The percentile-bootstrap interval there is not informative. When every
discordant pair favours A3, a resample can reach $\Delta\le 0$ only by drawing no discordant item
at all, so the lower bound is positive almost by construction. Only the $n=257$ run supports a
nonzero effect (20/0 discordant; the file rounds the exact McNemar $p$ to 0.0, i.e.\ $p<0.001$).
Both runs missed the registered $+8.0$ threshold, and we report both as FAIL.

The same file shows the v3 gate weakness that motivates v4. Of the answers the gate called
VERIFIED (coverage 80.93\,\%), only 88.94\,\% passed the hidden tests. By arithmetic on those two
file values (not a measurement), about 8.95\,\% of all problems were confident-wrong under v3.

\subsection{Defects found in the night data}\label{sec:defects}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Math prompt path (blocking).} All 26 math completions were produced with the Python
  code system prompt and a \verb|```python| prefill, because the prompt builder fell back to the
  \code{python} tag. The fix is in the code (D28). The 26 night items have not been regenerated on
  CPU. The math generations of Section~\ref{sec:etpu} use the fixed prompt.
\item \textbf{Unregistered math answer extraction (D24).} The v3.3.0 scorer fell back to the last
  number in the text. Since v3.3.1 the registered boxed-only rule is the default.
\item \textbf{Rust harness (D27).} No Rust program could link in the sandbox, and the pass nonce
  could be forged. Both are fixed and validated (25/25, 9/9).
\item \textbf{Python harness environment (D29).} Fixed; the Python result is unchanged.
\item \textbf{Gate not recorded at night.} The \code{gate} field is null in every night row.
\item \textbf{Hidden tests in the HumanEval+ gate payload (D25).} 11/11 E-night and 161/161 primary
  HumanEval+ gate payloads contained the hidden \code{inputs}/\code{results} lists. No reported
  number changes, because the L2 run executed no gate. The task files were rebuilt with the prompts
  and hidden checks unchanged. E-night has 80 items; primary E has 1,536 items after 5 fail-closed
  exclusions. All E-set runs in Section~\ref{sec:etpu} use the rebuilt \code{.d25} primary file.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What ``passed'' means in this paper.} An item is PASS\_HIDDEN only if it passed the named
hidden checks:
\begin{itemize}
\item Python: the full EvalPlus test suite, run in a bubblewrap sandbox.
\item Rust: compilation with \code{rustc}, then all \code{assert!}/\code{assert_eq!}/\code{assert_ne!}
  calls of the MultiPL-E test, run in a separate sandbox.
\item Math: sympy/numeric equivalence between the gold answer and the last \verb|\boxed{}| answer.
  If there is no \verb|\boxed|, the item is UNDECIDED and is not counted as correct.
\end{itemize}
It means nothing more.

comparison as risk--coverage curves.

Several caveats limit this result:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The gate executes a visible test, which is information the log-prob does not have. Part of
  the gap is simply ``running a test helps''. (Added in 3.9.0, D63: for the 373 MBPP tasks the gate's assert is printed in the prompt, so the model's log-prob is conditioned on that test; the gate executes it, the log-prob does not.)
\item The baseline answers the $k$ most confident tasks, with $k$ equal to the gate's answered
  count in the same domain (recomputed in each bootstrap resample). No outcome labels are used to
  choose $k$, and ties are broken pessimistically. $k$ is matched on E rather than taken from a
  threshold frozen on a separate split. We do not claim a direction for any resulting bias.
\item The benchmarks are public and probably in pretraining.
\item There is a single greedy sample.
\item This is \textbf{not} H1. H1 compares GL (gate plus Laya) with B3, whose threshold is set on a
  separate split C, and requires the coverage difference to lie within $\pm3$ points.

\paragraph{A5, the gate cascade (exploratory, not GL; Table~\ref{tab:e-cascade}, Figure~\ref{fig:e-pareto}).}

tasks: all 500 math tasks, 77 Python and 180 Rust. The ``cascade accuracy'' below is the hidden pass
rate of the final candidate, whether or not the arm answered. Its selective metrics are:
\begin{itemize}
\item pooled coverage 0.507 (CI 0.490--0.525) with confident-wrong rate 0.103 (CI 0.089--0.117);
\item Python 0.854 and 0.206;
\item Rust 0.645 and 0.097;
\item math coverage 0 (\code{A5.cascade_selective_*}; superseded: 3.5.0 pooled 0.669, CWR 0.113, math 0.496 and 0.030).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No accuracy gain.} Pooled accuracy is 0.604 for the cascade and 0.606 for the 4B, a
  difference of $-0.003$ (CI $-0.014$ to $0.009$). The paired exact McNemar test gives 40 tasks
  correct only under the cascade and 44 only under the 4B, $p=0.744$ (Holm 0.744). On Python the
  cascade is slightly worse: $-0.026$ (CI $-0.053$ to $-0.002$). The upper bound is within
  Monte-Carlo noise of 0 (the re-audit obtained 0.000), so this is borderline. Gate-verified 2B answers
  that fail the hidden checks are never escalated. On Rust the difference is $+0.020$ (CI $-0.004$
  to $0.043$). Against the 2B alone, the cascade gains 0.180 pooled (295 vs 18 discordant, Holm
  $p=2.0\times10^{-65}$). That gain comes from the 4B.
\item \textbf{Not cheaper than always-largest, pooled.} The pooled ratio of cascade to 4B
  chip-seconds is 1.357 (CI 1.329--1.385) with the first, compilation-bearing chunk counted, as
  planned. By domain it is 0.997 (CI 0.925--1.071) for Python, 1.221 for Rust and 1.514 for math.

  Excluding the first chunk of every model gives the post-hoc sensitivity S2
  (\code{S2.cost_ratio_cascade_over_large_nowarm.*}): pooled 1.243 (CI 1.224--1.260), Python 0.900
  (CI 0.834--0.965), Rust 1.090 and math 1.398. Pooled, the cascade is more expensive either way. For
  Python it breaks even with warm-up and is about 10\% cheaper without it, at a 2.6-point accuracy
  cost. The cascade escalates 0.675 of tasks pooled: 0.408 on Python, 0.633 on Rust and
  every math task. It therefore pays for the 2B and then for the 4B on most tasks. The cost per
  correct answer is 0.821 chip-seconds for the cascade (CI 0.759--0.889) and 0.603 for the 4B (CI
  0.563--0.646).
\item \textbf{Ceiling (A6).} An oracle that picks a correct tier whenever one exists would reach 0.644 pooled
  (CI 0.620--0.668), against 0.606 for the 4B. By domain it reaches 0.707 / 0.574 / 0.648
  (Python / Rust / math), against 0.675 / 0.529 / 0.612. Any router between \emph{these two} tiers
  can gain at most these differences in accuracy on these tasks. The oracle is a bound only and
  never a comparator.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Cost caveats.} The chip-seconds measure throughput at each model's engine concurrency, not latency.
They are not comparable to the registered L4 GPU-seconds. Three points matter here:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The 4B ran at an effective concurrency of at most 11, against at most 54 for the 2B
  (configured 32 and 96). This makes the 4B dearer per token, so the measured cascade cost penalty
  is \emph{understated} relative to an equal-concurrency setup.
\item The slow first chunk is a larger share of the 2B's total (144 of 297\,s) than of the 4B's (192
  of 561\,s). S2 above removes it.
\item The CPU time of the gate itself is not counted.
\end{enumerate}
Cost is therefore configuration dependent. The ratios above are not robust estimates of any
deployment cost, and we draw no cost conclusion beyond ``not cheaper pooled, in this
configuration''. The accuracy result does not depend on cost.

\subsection{A10: the program-of-thought math gate (revision 3.5.0; post hoc, exploratory)}\label{sec:a10}

\paragraph{What changed.} The math-gap statement of 3.4.0 is superseded (Section~\ref{sec:corr350}). The registered
design (registration Section~1) is now implemented in \code{gwaya/domains/math_gate.py} and specified in addendum A10
of \code{docs/ANALYSIS_PLAN_E_TPU.md}, which was written before any program was generated and after the E baselines had
been seen. The tier that answered writes a short Python program that computes the answer and prints it. The program
runs in the same bubblewrap sandbox as the code checks (no network, no host files, time and memory limits). The gate
VERIFIES iff the program's last output line is equivalent to the answer's final \verb|\boxed{}| value, with the same
equivalence as the scorer. The gold answer is never an input. A missing boxed answer, a missing program, a crash, a
timeout, unparseable output or a mismatch all give UNVERIFIED (a wrong program does not refute the answer). One greedy
program per tier and math task, at most 1,024 new tokens, same TPU, engine and settings as the answers.

\paragraph{Cost.} The program is a second accelerator generation. Every gated arm (gate-only and the cascade) is charged
the program's attributed chip-seconds for each tier it consults; ungated arms are not. The program run and this
charge are why the math cascade is dearer than in 3.4.0.

\paragraph{Gate-only (Table~\ref{tab:a10-gate}, Figure~\ref{fig:a10-rc}).} Numbers are in the table
(\code{A3.*}, \code{A4.*} of \code{numbers_e_tpu_mathgate.json}). On the 4B the gate verifies 0.454 of the math answers
and 0.943 of those pass the hidden check, so the confident-wrong rate over all math tasks is 0.026. \textbf{Corrected in 3.9.0:} the 3.5.0 text set this against 0.388 ``when
every answer is given'', but that baseline counts the 160 tasks with no boxed answer (160 of the 194 wrong rows; mostly truncated) as confident-wrong. Answering whenever a boxed answer exists gives 0.068 at coverage 0.680 (registered scorer; Table~\ref{tab:mathfix}), so against that baseline the gate removes 0.042 of confident-wrong answers over all tasks at a cost of 0.226 in coverage. At the same coverage, ``answer the most confident'' gives a higher rate; the difference is $-0.084$.
Pooled over all 1,536 tasks with coverage matched within each domain, the gate's rate is 0.078 against 0.169 for the
baseline ($-0.090$, CI $-0.104$ to $-0.079$; \code{A4.*.domain_matched}). The 2B's math gate verifies fewer answers
(0.288) at precision 0.951. Python and Rust are unchanged from 3.4.0.

Reading and caveats.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The program is written by the same tier that wrote the answer. A match is agreement between two outputs of one
  model, not independent ground truth. Precision 0.943 means that a share of verified math answers still fail the
  hidden check; its point estimate is below the registration's 0.95 flag point and its CI (0.911--0.971) includes
  it. This is a descriptive analogue, not an H7 result.
\item Coverage 0.454 means that 0.546 of the 4B's math answers stay UNVERIFIED for any of the reasons above. We did
  not break the cause down.
\item As in A4, the gate runs a program that the log-prob does not see, $k$ is matched on E, the benchmarks are public,
  and there is a single greedy sample. This is not H1.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Exploratory re-score of the math labels (revision 3.9.0; D62; Table~\ref{tab:mathfix}).} After this section was written, a review of the stored math answers found that the registered scorer (\code{gwaya/domains/math_check.py} at \code{78cf88b}) marks some equivalent answers wrong: brace-less \verb|\frac14| and \verb|\sqrt2|, units or a leading \verb|x =| on the gold answer only, base subscripts, spacing inside tuples, intervals and matrices, and mixed numbers. The scorer was changed to normalize these and to stay fail-closed on anything undecidable (two drafts were rejected by independent reviews for fail-open paths; commits \code{f837c9f}, \code{2305d90}, \code{5e14bd8}). \code{scripts/rescore_math_stored.py} scored every stored (tier, task) with both scorers (the registered one reproduces all 1,500 stored labels) and re-ran each program once. Because the rules were designed after seeing these outputs, \textbf{the re-score is exploratory and post hoc; the registered values in this section and in Table~\ref{tab:a10-gate} remain the primary record}. What it shows: 22, 26 and 25 wrong-to-right flips (2B, 4B, 9B), none right-to-wrong, all notation equivalences; math accuracy 0.558, 0.664 and 0.692 instead of 0.514, 0.612 and 0.642. The math gate's VERIFIED-but-wrong count falls from 7, 13 and 10 to 2, 0 and 0 of 500: on the 4B and 9B every remaining verified answer is correct (rule-of-three 95\% upper bounds 0.006 over all tasks and 0.013 among verified), and the 2B keeps two genuinely wrong answers that its program agreed with. So most of the 4B gate's apparent misses below the 0.95 precision flag were scorer false negatives, under a scorer change we made after looking. Against the answer-if-boxed baseline (0.016 and 0.002 under the re-score), the 4B and 9B gates remove only small absolute amounts of confident-wrong answers on E, at a coverage cost of about a third of the boxed answers; E's math is easy for these tiers, so a zero here is not evidence that the gate has no confident-wrong answers in general. The re-score was not propagated into A4 (the math difference $-0.084$ and the pooled $-0.090$), A5, A11, A12 or the Laya labels.

\subsection{A11: cross-fitted calibrated abstention (revision 3.5.0; post hoc, exploratory)}\label{sec:a11}

Addendum A11 (written before any model was fitted) replaces a calibrator fitted on the non-existent split C with a fully
pre-specified cross-fitted procedure run once on E. It is \emph{not} the registered Laya calibrator and not H4, H5 or H6.
Features come from stored outputs only (gate verdict, mean, minimum and lowest-10\% token log-probability, log token count,
truncation flag, domain); the gold answer and the hidden checks are never inputs. Folds are 5, by source problem
(deterministic hash). M0 is the gate verdict alone, M1 uses the mean log-prob only, M2 all non-gate features and M3 all
features, the last three as logistic regressions evaluated only on out-of-fold predictions
(\code{scripts/analyze_abstention.py}; \code{abstention_a11.json}). The 4B is the headline tier and the 2B secondary. A11
was not run for the 9B.

\paragraph{Discrimination (Table~\ref{tab:a11-disc-4B}).} On the 4B, pooled AUROC is 0.851 (M0), 0.708 (M1) and 0.931 (M3);
pooled AURC (lower is better) is 0.256 for M1 and 0.135 for M3. M3 beats M1 on AUROC by 0.223 (CI 0.197--0.249) and
M0 by 0.079 (CI 0.064--0.095) (\code{disc.d_auroc.*.pooled}); per domain the M3$-$M0 AUROC differences are 0.094 (Python),
0.029 (Rust) and 0.119 (math), all with CIs above zero. On the 2B pooled AUROC is 0.958 for M3 against 0.703 for M1.
\textbf{M3 uses the gate verdict as a feature, so its advantage over the log-prob score is mostly calibration of the gate
information and not independent evidence that a learned score is better than the gate.} Raw confidence was far worse
calibrated (ECE 0.252--0.402 per domain on the 4B, Table~\ref{tab:e-calib}) than the out-of-fold M3 (0.025--0.045).

\paragraph{Operating points (Table~\ref{tab:a11-op}).} With target selective risk $\alpha=0.05$ the log-prob-only M1 never
met the inner-fold rule (coverage 0 in every domain and pooled), while M3 answers 0.275 of tasks pooled (CI 0.254--0.298)
at realised risk 0.052 (CI 0.031--0.074). At $\alpha=0.10$ M3 answers 0.585 pooled at realised risk 0.101 (CI 0.082--0.122);
M1 answers 0.033 at risk 0.220 (CI 0.109--0.339). The gate alone answers 0.624 at risk 0.125 (CI 0.105--0.146). The realised
risk of M3 at $\alpha=0.05$ is 0.043 (Python), 0.037 (Rust) and 0.066 (math), with CIs that cover 0.05 in each case. We
claim no pass/fail on ``meeting the target''; the CIs are wide and the plan says so in advance.

\paragraph{Caveats.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item CIs resample source-problem clusters of \emph{fixed} out-of-fold predictions. They do not include variability of the
  model fit.
\item ECE percentile-bootstrap CIs sometimes exclude their own point estimate. For example, for M2 on 4B Python the point
  estimate is 0.056 and the CI 0.057--0.111 (\code{disc.ece.M2.python}); the same happens for M2 on Rust (0.045, CI
  0.047--0.101). This is the known upward bias of ECE under resampling, so the CIs of ECE are not read as symmetric
  intervals around the estimate.
\item It is one run on the burned E set, public benchmarks, one greedy sample, and not the registered GL.
\item (Added in 3.9.0.) E is burned for H1/H3, the
benchmarks are public, there is a single greedy sample, and nothing here involves Laya.

\subsection{What was built}

\paragraph{Fresh pool P.} Pool P is disjoint from E. It holds 1,500 problems sampled from the MATH train split
(7,496 available) and 600 from GSM8K train (7,473 available), and 556 MBPP tasks whose id and prompt are not in MBPP+
(\code{pool_manifest.json}: 974 raw MBPP tasks, 378 dropped because their task id is in MBPP+, 2 because their prompt

(Table~\ref{tab:laya-data}). The seed is 20261009. Source dataset revisions are recorded in the manifest. Four MATH problems
with no boxed answer were dropped. Rust has no fresh pool here (MultiPL-E Rust is all in E), so \textbf{nothing about Rust
is evaluated on E$'$}; the fresh Rust set R$'$ of revision 3.7.0 is a separate evaluation-only set (Section~\ref{sec:rfresh}).

\paragraph{Splits.} By cluster: $\mathrm{sha256(cluster)} \bmod 10$ in 0--5 train, 6 calibration split C, 7--9 evaluation set E$'$
(D48; the reserved 20-bucket rule would have left about 10\% of a small pool for training). The training set is P train plus
\emph{all} of E (D49), i.e.\ 3,081 tasks and 9,243 (task, tier) calibrator rows. C has 283 tasks (849 rows) and is used only to
fit an isotonic map. E$'$ has 828 tasks (659 math, 169 Python; 2,484 rows). Tier labels (correct against the hidden check; gate

\input{tables_laya360.tex}

\paragraph{Which model ``Laya'' is here.} The backbone is \code{answerdotai/ModernBERT-large} with a LoRA adapter of
rank 16 on \code{Wqkv}, \code{Wo} and \code{Wi}, maximum length 512 (\code{head_config.json}, sha256 in
Section~\ref{sec:repro}); the HF revision of the backbone is not recorded in that file. There are two heads. The
\emph{router} head predicts, from the prompt alone ($n_{\text{features}}=0$), the probability that each of the three tiers is
correct. The \emph{calibrator} head predicts P(correct) for one (prompt, candidate answer, tier) row and also receives 16
numeric features built from the gate verdict and the signals \code{tests_passed_frac}, \code{selftest_agreement},
\code{mean_logprob}, \code{min_logprob} and \code{repair_rounds} (I did not audit which of these are non-constant in the data).
It therefore sees the gate and the log-prob. Training used torch\_xla 2.8 and peft on one TPU v5e chip: 3 epochs, learning
rate $2\times10^{-4}$, batch 16, seed 1234, bf16 autocast (A13b). The calibrator run took 1,566 steps (1,170.0\,s wall) and the router run
522 steps (448.8\,s), with torch 2.8.0+cu128, transformers 5.19.0 and peft 0.21.2 recorded in the run manifests. On the 10\% task-grouped
validation part of the training data (not C, not E$'$) the calibrator had AUROC 0.927 (n = 903) and the router 0.828 / 0.807 / 0.807
for the 2B / 4B / 9B (n = 301). The isotonic map has 38 knots and was fitted on C (n = 849). \textbf{This is not a model
named ``Laya'' that was pretrained elsewhere: the data-lake ``laya'' weights were not used.} No generator
adapter exists (Section~\ref{sec:laya-neg}).

\paragraph{Freeze order.} The sha256 of the adapters, heads, head configurations, C predictions and isotonic map are in

\subsection{Confirmatory family on E$'$: H1 and H3, with deviations stated}\label{sec:laya-h}

The family was fixed in A13b: Holm over $\{$H1, H3$\}$ on the primary tier (Qwen3.5-9B), math and Python pooled; per tier as
secondary. \textbf{These are tests with three recorded deviations that every reading must carry:}
D48 (the split above, not the registered 20-bucket rule), D49 (E, which was already burned for these tests, is in the training set;
E$'$ shares no cluster, task id or prompt hash with the training data) and D50 (the registered shuffled-label control
failed and was withdrawn, Section~\ref{sec:laya-ctrl}). The metrics are those of A13b (AURC for H1, selective risk at the gate's own
coverage for H3), not the registered CWR-at-matched-coverage metric and McNemar test of the registration; this is a further difference
from the registered form.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1 is supported on E$'$ (math and Python)} (Table~\ref{tab:laya-h}): on the 9B the AURC of the calibrated Laya P(correct) is lower than that of the raw
  mean log-prob by $0.078$ (95\% CI $-0.106$ to $-0.053$; Holm-adjusted $p=0.0002$, which is the floor of a 10,000-resample bootstrap). The same
  holds for the 4B ($-0.092$) and the 2B ($-0.136$).
\item \textbf{H3 is not supported on any tier}: the selective risk of Laya at the gate's own coverage differs from the gate's by $-0.012$
  (CI $-0.023$ to $+0.011$) on the 9B, $-0.010$ (CI $-0.024$ to $+0.016$) on the 4B and $+0.018$ (CI $-0.026$ to $+0.039$) on the 2B.
  All CIs include zero. This does not show that Laya and the gate are equal, only that E$'$ cannot separate them.
\end{itemize}
The raw log-prob is a weak comparator: its ECE, taking $\exp(\text{mean log-prob})$ as the probability, is 0.243 on the 9B (0.275 on the 4B, 0.336 on the 2B), against 0.033, 0.028 and 0.050 for the calibrated Laya (Table~\ref{tab:laya-metrics}). The exponentiated mean log-prob is a confidence that is not calibrated by construction, so the ECE comparison says little about ranking; H1 is on AURC, which is rank-based.

\paragraph{Exploratory: Laya against a logistic regression on hand-built features.} A logistic regression on the gate verdict, mean and
minimum log-prob, domain and tier, fitted on the same training rows, is about as good as Laya (Tables~\ref{tab:laya-metrics}, \ref{tab:laya-lr}).
Laya's AUROC is higher by $0.013$ on the 9B (CI $0.001$ to $0.026$) and by $0.015$ on the 4B (CI $0.003$ to $0.029$), and not distinguishable on the 2B
($0.004$, CI $-0.011$ to $0.020$). These gains are below the $+0.02$ minimum effect that the registration states for H5 (H5 is not tested here; its LR and
its data differ). On AURC, Laya's point estimates are \emph{higher} (worse) than the LR's on all three tiers ($+0.008$ on the 9B) and every CI includes zero.
My reading: most of Laya's advantage over the raw log-prob and over the gate alone comes from combining the gate verdict with the log-prob signals, which the
calibrator receives and which the LR also uses; as for the text of the prompt and answer: a text-only calibrator (3.8.0, Section~\ref{sec:textonly}) is predictive on E$'$ (AUROC 0.84 to 0.87) and at chance on R$'$, and these data do not show what the text adds on top of the gate and log-prob inputs (no ablation removed the text from the full model). This reading is consistent with the numbers but is not a tested decomposition.

\subsection{The shuffled-label control failed, and what was done about it}\label{sec:laya-ctrl}

A13b registered a control: heads trained identically on shuffled labels (SH) must give AUROC between 0.4 and 0.6 on C, otherwise ``the
pipeline is declared broken and E$'$ is not generated''. They did not (Table~\ref{tab:laya-ctrl}). The SH calibrator scored 0.693 pooled
(0.553 to 0.692 within the six domain $\times$ tier strata) and the SH router 0.572 / 0.637 / 0.389 for the 2B / 4B / 9B. We then scored the untrained, same-seed twin of
each head on C: the calibrator twin scored 0.480 pooled but 0.239 to 0.514 within strata, and the router twin 0.653 / 0.482 / 0.575. The Spearman correlation between the SH and twin
calibrator scores is 0.136, so the SH value is not an initialisation effect. The trained heads score 0.952 (calibrator) and 0.780 / 0.808 / 0.821 (router).

\textbf{The criterion was withdrawn (D50) and replaced by a direct leakage check; applied as written, it would have stopped the pipeline and E$'$ would not
have been generated.} E$'$ was generated after D50 was committed. The replacement check found no overlap between E$'$ and the training data by cluster, task id or normalised prompt hash
(0 / 0 / 0), and between C and the training data 0 / 0 / 1 (one prompt-hash match, in C only; D49). The reading we offer for the failed control is that a head fed
the strongly predictive gate and log-prob features, or a router scored pooled over domains with different base rates, ranks by those inputs in an arbitrary direction
when it has learned nothing from the labels, so the criterion cannot be met reliably by a correct pipeline. \textbf{This reading is not proven.} We did not test it
(for example, by repeating the SH run with several seeds or by dropping the numeric features), and the SH result remains an unexplained failure of a registered
check. A reader who does not accept the withdrawal should treat the E$'$ results as obtained without a passed leakage control.

\subsection{Exploratory router policy}\label{sec:laya-router}

The router policy starts each task at the cheapest tier whose router P(correct) is at least $\tau$ (else at the 9B), then climbs the tiers and answers at the first tier whose gate verifies
(A13b). $\tau$ was chosen on C from $\{0.5,\dots,0.9\}$ as the cheapest value whose C accuracy is at least that of the static ladder (Table~\ref{tab:laya-tau}); only $\tau=0.5$
met the target on C, and 0.5 is the lowest value in the grid, so lower values were not tried. On E$'$ (Table~\ref{tab:laya-router}):
\begin{itemize}
\item accuracy is 0.705 against 0.696 for always-9B (difference $+0.010$, CI $0.001$ to $0.018$) and 0.704 for the static ladder ($+0.001$, CI $-0.008$ to $0.012$);
\item the confident-wrong rate is 4.0\% (CI 2.7--5.3\%) against 30.4\% for always-9B. That comparison is not like for like: always-9B answers every task, while the router answers
  only where a gate verifies. The static ladder's rate is 6.6\%;
\item cost per task is $0.79$ of always-9B's with the first compile chunk of each run included (CI 0.75--0.83) and $0.89$ (CI 0.866--0.924) with each run's first 192-task chunk set to zero
  (\code{router_nowarm.json}). Against the static ladder the ratios are $0.76$ and $0.71$. Cost is list-price chip-seconds, with the 9B counted at four chips and its concurrency not measured (D45).
\end{itemize}
This is exploratory: E$'$ is mostly easy math (GSM8K and easy MATH), so accuracies are higher than on E and the router may find it easy to start low; the cost advantage need not hold on harder tasks.

\subsection{Revision 3.9.0: gate strength and the verdict policy (system changes)}\label{sec:policy390}

These are code changes, not results. \code{gwaya/domains/strength.py} and \code{checkers._apply_strength} (\code{da7d708}) record with every Python and Rust gate verdict how many visible tests stood behind it (\code{visible_tests}: Python, the number of input/result pairs of a generated suite or else of \code{assert} statements; Rust, \code{\#[test]} functions or else assert macros, after stripping comments and strings, fixed in \code{dfd41aa}). The count is a static lower bound and says nothing about test quality. A task payload may set \code{min_visible_tests}; a pass below it is then UNVERIFIED (fail-closed). \code{gwaya/gwenlaya.py} (\code{dfd41aa}) adds a verdict policy, written into every output's evidence:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Weak gate} (Python, Rust; \textbf{off by default}). With $k_{\min}$ configured for a domain (argument or \code{GWENLAYA_MIN_VISIBLE_TESTS}), a gate pass backed by fewer than $k_{\min}$ visible tests, or by an unknown count, is not reported as VERIFIED; it takes the calibrator path of an unverified answer (LIKELY\_CORRECT, LIKELY\_WRONG only through the low threshold, or escalate/abstain). No $k_{\min}$ has been chosen, because no held-out data exist for choosing it.
\item \textbf{Lean.} A Lean task without a non-empty formal statement is never VERIFIED (the checker could not tell the task's theorem from an easier one the model wrote), and a Lean answer is never LIKELY\_CORRECT (the frozen Laya never saw Lean, so only the kernel can vouch).
\end{itemize}
\input{tables_policy390.tex}

\textbf{What the stored data say (exploratory, not pre-registered; Table~\ref{tab:policy390}).} \code{scripts/verdict_policy_exploratory.py} counted the visible tests in each stored gate payload. Every VERIFIED of the stored E gate, Python and Rust, all tiers, rests on exactly one visible test; on R$'$ three verified answers (one 4B, two 9B) rest on more. So any $k_{\min}\ge2$ would demote every stored E VERIFIED to the calibrator path, and on R$'$ keep three. The table's ``wrong among verified'' is read from the stored rows without the serial-recheck overlay of A8, so it can differ by one task from Table~\ref{tab:e-gate} (4B Python: 0.172 here, 0.170 from the published precision 0.830). Nothing here shows that the policy lowers confident-wrong answers for a user: a user supplies their own tests, and E, which set no default, is an evaluation set. What A15.2 and A17 suggest is only that more visible tests make VERIFIED more trustworthy on these answers. None of the published results uses the policy. Because it changes what VERIFIED means when it is switched on, it is recorded as deviation D64.

\subsection{Revision 3.9.0: the Lean 4 gate (system changes; no results)}\label{sec:lean390}

The registration (Section~1) lists a Lean~4 gate (``\code{lake env lean} kernel check + \code{\#print axioms} audit; precision = 1 by construction'') and miniF2F-lean4 (244 statements) as a secondary set. Lean was dropped from the night run (D7) and has produced no result in any revision.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The gate was unusable.} Two defects made every miniF2F proof impossible to verify: the checker never passed the pinned Mathlib project (Lean v4.7.0, Mathlib \code{a45ae637}) to the oracle, so every Mathlib import was UNVERIFIED (\code{aa572bb}); and the loader kept the dataset's trailing \verb|:= sorry| inside the statement the gate requires verbatim, and \code{sorry} is rejected (\code{d97e390}).
\item \textbf{Soundness holes.} An internal audit of the repaired gate (its findings are in the A18 amendment, \code{f318f71}, and reproduced as tests; no separate report is committed) found that ``precision 1 by construction'' did not hold: the statement check was a raw substring test (the statement inside a comment or a string, next to an easier theorem, passed); \code{variable} hypotheses, local notation and instances and namespace shadowing could change the meaning of an intact statement; a \code{\#eval} hidden in a string could print fake axiom-audit lines; and a task with no formal statement accepted any compiling file.
\item \textbf{The hardened gate} (\code{gwaya/lean_gate.py}, \code{24bc5c0}). The reference (task header, formal statement, \verb|by sorry|) is compiled alone; the candidate is compiled separately; a \emph{trusted second Lean process} with fixed source reads both compiled modules as data, replays every candidate declaration through the kernel, and accepts only if the target is a theorem whose type equals the reference type (exact expression equality, or else kernel definitional equality; which one is recorded) and whose \code{collectAxioms} closure is within \code{propext}, \code{Classical.choice} and \code{Quot.sound}. Its output lines carry a per-run random nonce, so candidate output cannot spoof them. Lean-aware lexical rules (comments and strings stripped first) reject \code{variable}, notation, instances, attributes, namespaces, meta-programming, IO and escape hatches. \code{tests/test_lean_gate.py} reproduces each audit exploit; in my run for this revision all 94 tests passed, including the 13 that use the real pinned project (four genuine miniF2F proofs VERIFIED with an exact match, a wrong proof FAILED, and each exploit rejected by the trusted process alone with the lexical rules bypassed; \code{results/gwenlaya_v4/lean_a18/lean_gate_tests_390.log}).
\item \textbf{Limits of the hardened gate.} (i) Binder annotations (explicit or implicit arguments) are not compared: both the expression equality and the definitional-equality check ignore them. (ii) A definitional (not exact) match counts as VERIFIED; A18 results will report exact and definitional matches separately. (iii) The external red-team review of the hardened gate was not completed. (iv) The strict lexical rules reject some harmless constructs (for example any \code{namespace}, \code{variable} or local instance in a proof), so some valid proofs will be FAILED. (v) A check is slow: in my run a VERIFIED genuine proof took 12 to 21\,s per check at a load average of about 3 on 8 cores (log above); it will be longer under heavier load. No proof has yet been scored with it.
\item \textbf{A18.} Plan addendum A18 (\code{5b53ce8}, before any Lean generation) and its amendment (\code{f318f71}, before any Lean generation; score only with the hardened gate) pre-register a descriptive Lean miniF2F run with the 2B, 4B and 9B tiers (greedy, and $k=8$ samples). The sanity check (\code{lean_sanity.json}) keeps 223 of 244 statements: 17 are commented out in the dataset and 4 do not elaborate under the pinned Mathlib. The generation began after the amendment and is in progress (the $k=8$ sampling is being re-run after an engine-seed fix, \code{302ab95}). \textbf{No Lean result is reported in this revision.} Its ledger lines are counted in Section~\ref{sec:spend}. Replacing the registered Lean check by this gate is deviation D65.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reading (hypotheses; none was tested by an ablation of its cause).}
(i) In distribution the encoder reads something predictive of correctness from the prompt and candidate text. The converse ablation (numeric inputs only, no text) is essentially the hand-feature regression, which is a different model; no causal decomposition was run.

\textbf{Revision 3.6.0: H1 and H3 were run on a fresh E$'$ (math, Python) with deviations (Section~\ref{sec:laya-h}); revision 3.7.0 repeated them with the same frozen Laya on a fresh Rust set R$'$, where neither is supported (Section~\ref{sec:rfresh}).} The following paragraph, from 3.5.0, explains why the E generations themselves cannot be used.

\textbf{H1 and H3 cannot be run as registered on these E generations.} The registration's freeze rule
(Section~6) requires the calibrator, all thresholds and the E manifest hash to be fixed in a signed
addendum \emph{before E is generated}, and it forbids E-based refitting. E has now been generated, and
its outcomes analysed and published, before split C or the Laya head exist. A registered test
therefore needs one of two things:
\begin{itemize}
\item fresh generations on a new evaluation set E$'$ after the freeze (for example, problems released
  after the models' training cutoff, which would also reduce contamination); or
\item a recorded deviation stating that the E generations predate the freeze and that their outcomes
  were seen, in which case any H1/H3 run on them is a deviated test.
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}

  tautological'').

\section{TPU feasibility findings}\label{sec:tpu}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Qwen2.5 works.} In smoke run \#1 (on demand, us-central1-a; logs under
  \url{gs://socrateai-datalake-gen-lang-client-0625573011/gwenlaya_v4/tpu_smoke/gwenlaya-tpu-smoke-215529/}),
  \code{Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct} loaded and generated with vLLM 0.31.0's TPU backend: load
  208.9\,s, then 442 completion tokens in 0.61\,s. The gate on that VM returned UNVERIFIED (no
  bubblewrap there), which is the designed fail-closed behaviour.
\item \textbf{Qwen3.5 0.8B, 2B and 4B generate on one v5e chip with lazy compilation.} In run \#1,
  \code{Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B} did not finish loading within 1,500\,s. Qwen3.5 is not JAX-native in
  tpu-inference and runs through the PyTorch fallback. It runs when up-front precompilation is
  skipped (\code{SKIP_JAX_PRECOMPILE=1}). With that setting:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Qwen3.5-0.8B generated 16 night tasks in run \#2
    (\code{results/gwenlaya_v4/tpu_run2/res_qwen35_0p8b.jsonl.summary.json}). The run \#2 scores
    are not used, because its TPU-host gate self-test reported \code{python_ok False}.
  \item Qwen3.5-2B and 4B generated all 80 night tasks in run \#3.
  \item The same two models generated all 1,536 E tasks in this revision.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Concurrency limit for the 4B.} On the 16\,GiB chip the 4B (hybrid full/linear
  attention; vLLM sizes a ``mamba'' recurrent-state pool) failed to start with \code{max_num_seqs} 96
  (``Cannot fit both KV pools''). It started at 32, where the engine re-split the KV cache for 11
  concurrent requests. The 2B ran at 96 configured and 54 effective (Section~\ref{sec:etpu}).
\item \textbf{Log-probabilities need \code{logprobs=1}.} The first E pass returned none
  (Section~\ref{sec:etpu}). The committed generator requests \code{logprobs=1} and fails fast if
  none come back.
\item \textbf{No TPU LoRA path was found for Qwen3.5 dense or Qwen3.8} in the stacks we inspected.
  Serving Qwen2.5 with \code{enable_lora} failed at engine initialisation, and this is the only
  vLLM-TPU LoRA attempt. Tunix 0.1.7 lists Qwen2/Qwen3; MaxText lists Qwen2.5/Qwen3 and Qwen3.5
  mixture-of-experts scripts. (3.5.0 concluded that the Laya head and SFT would therefore stay on the GPU/CPU plan; in 3.6.0 the Laya head was in fact trained on the TPU with torch\_xla and peft, Section~\ref{sec:laya}; a torch\_xla and peft LoRA probe of Qwen3.5-0.8B finished no step in 25 minutes.)
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No registered-form test.} The only tests of registered hypotheses (H1, H3 on E$'$, 3.6.0, and on the Rust set R$'$, 3.7.0) are deviated (D48--D50, and D53--D56 for R$'$, and D58) and use the A13b metrics. Nothing here supports or refutes
  H2, H4--H12, H14 or H15.
\item \textbf{Unregistered E-set baselines.} The full-E baselines are exploratory: single greedy
  sample, one chip, one engine version, bf16, two tiers, weights not pinned to a commit. Four of the
  plan's analysis choices were amended after the first results (Section~\ref{sec:plan}). The Coder
  rows existed 10\,s before the plan commit.
\item \textbf{Matched coverage in A4.} The baseline's $k$ is matched to the gate's coverage on E
  rather than taken from a threshold frozen on a separate split. The gate's advantage also includes
  executing a visible test.
\item \textbf{E is spent for registered H1/H3.} E was generated and analysed before the
  registration's freeze (calibrator and thresholds), so H1/H3 cannot be run as registered on these
  generations. A registered test needs fresh generations on a new set E$'$, or a recorded deviation.
\item \textbf{The cascade is not GL.} It has no Laya, is compared with B2 rather than B5, and is
  not costed in GPU-seconds. Its chip-second cost depends on engine concurrency (effective at most
  11 for the 4B vs at most 54 for the 2B) and on whether the compilation chunk is counted (pooled
  ratio 2.021 with it, 2.023 without, with the math gate of 3.5.0 and its program charge; 1.357 and 1.243 in 3.4.0), and it excludes the CPU
  time of the gate.
\item \textbf{Math gate (revision 3.5.0).} The math gate (program-of-thought re-execution) is now
  implemented (A10), replacing the 3.4.0 ``not implemented'' statement. It is post hoc, the program is written by
  the same tier as the answer (so verification is agreement of one model with itself), its 4B precision 0.943 is
  below the 0.95 flag point (CI includes it), and its cost is charged only to gated arms. (3.9.0) Its headline comparison with ``answer everything'' (0.026 against 0.388) mostly reflects abstaining on truncated non-answers; against answer-if-boxed it is 0.026 against 0.068. Math accuracy is still a
  lower bound, because up to 40\,\% of math answers (2B) were truncated at 1,024 tokens.
\item \textbf{9B tier and chip-seconds (A12).} The 9B ran on a four-chip slice and its effective engine concurrency was
  not measured, so its cost, and every ratio against always-9B, is less certain. The ``pays off'' verdicts use point
  estimates for cost and have no multiplicity correction. The 27B was attempted in 3.6.0 and did not finish loading (Section~\ref{sec:laya-neg}). A11 was not run for the 9B.
\item \textbf{A11 (calibrated abstention).} M3 uses the gate verdict as a feature, so its gain is calibration and not
  independent evidence. CIs do not include model-fit variability, and ECE percentile-bootstrap CIs can exclude their own
  point estimates. It is not the Laya head of 3.6.0.
\item \textbf{Post hoc.} A10, A11 and A12 were all decided after the E baselines had been seen (D43--D47), on an
  evaluation set that is burned for the registered H1/H3.
\item \textbf{Stability.} One re-run of each passing row cannot rule out unstable candidates.
  Contention and five genuine timeouts per model remain. Scoring ran from working trees with
  uncommitted changes.
\item \textbf{Contamination.} All absolute scores are on public sets that Qwen3.5 has probably
  seen. Only paired within-model deltas are claimed by the registration. The contamination-stratified
  analysis asked for in review finding F12 was not done (\TBD).
\item \textbf{Night data.} The night math generations are invalid (D28). The Rust harness was
  validated after the night generations existed, on hand-written solutions (25 of 26 tasks covered).
  The HumanEval+ night gate payloads contained the hidden tests (D25), but no gate ran there.
\item \textbf{Laya and E$'$ (3.6.0).} E$'$ has 169 Python tasks and mostly easy math and no Rust; nothing is claimed about Rust from E$'$ (the Rust test is R$'$, next item); one greedy sample; list-price cost estimates; E is in the Laya training set (D49); the calibrator receives the gate and log-prob as inputs, so its advantage over each alone may largely be a combination effect (a reading only; the 3.8.0 text-only ablation shows the text alone is predictive on E$'$ but does not test what the text adds to the gate and log-prob); the shuffled-label control failed and its withdrawal rests on an unproven reading (D50); H1/H3 use the A13b metrics; the backbone revision is not pinned; no generator adapter exists; the data-lake ``laya'' weights were not used. The router policy is exploratory with $\tau$ at the lowest grid value; the 4.0\% against 30.4\% confident-wrong comparison is not like for like.
\item \textbf{Fresh Rust set R$'$ (3.7.0).} 69 tasks (wide intervals; H1 and H3 may simply be underpowered, and the null result is not evidence of no effect); the tasks come from a public repository and may be in pre-training data; they were kept by a reference and stub check with the same harness and built after the E$'$ results were known (D53, D54); one greedy sample; list-price costs; $\tau$ reused from C (D56). The reasons for the non-transfer of Laya's advantage (prompt style and length, 512-token truncation, Rust only from MultiPL-E in training, small $n$) are hypotheses and were not tested; input truncation was not measured. \textbf{Harness:} all published Rust results used edition 2015 (D55). A15.1 found at most one confirmed fail-to-pass flip per tier (E 1/507 for the 4B and the 9B, R$'$ 4B 1/69), so the effect is small and no number was re-analysed; the oracle default is now 2021 (D58), so future runs differ from the published ones in that respect.
\item \textbf{Text-only ablation (3.8.0).} Exploratory, one seed, post hoc after the E$'$ and R$'$ results (D59, D60); the in-distribution signal may reflect source or difficulty rather than the candidate (untested); R$'$ has 69 tasks, so ``at chance'' means ``not distinguishable from 0.5 here''; the reasons for the non-transfer are hypotheses; no shuffled-label control for this model; the converse ablation (no text) was not run.
\item \textbf{Depth dials (A15.2, A17).} Descriptive, on stored answers of E, 4B and 9B only: Rust 505 tasks, $k\le3$, edition 2015, deviating from A15 ($k=1,2,4$; D58); Python 524 tasks, $k\le8$ (3.9.0). Not run on R$'$ or E$'$. For MBPP the first visible test is the one printed in the prompt (D63), so the one-test gate partly checks a test the model saw. They say nothing about what a model would write given more visible tests, and they are not hypothesis tests.
\item \textbf{Math scorer (D62, 3.9.0).} The registered math labels contain scorer false negatives on notational equivalences; the corrected scorer was designed after inspecting the stored outputs, so the re-scored accuracies and gate counts are exploratory. A4, A5, A11, A12, the Laya training labels and E$'$ were not re-scored, so every math-dependent number there carries the registered scorer's misses.
\item \textbf{Verdict policy and Lean gate (3.9.0).} System changes without results. The weak-gate policy is off by default and has no chosen $k_{\min}$; its effect for real users is unknown. The hardened Lean gate does not compare binder annotations, accepts definitional matches, rejects some harmless constructs, takes tens of seconds per check, and has not had its external red-team review.
\item \textbf{Spend.} The USD spend (\$\SpendTotal, \SpendLines{} ledger lines, including the A18 Lean generation in progress) is a list-price estimate, not the billed amount. H13 is weak
  evidence (Section~\ref{sec:h13}).
\item \textbf{Not run.} The SFT and DPO training arms, quantization and the endpoint did not run. Lean~4 has no result: the gate was repaired and hardened and A18 is pre-registered (223 of 244 statements pass the sanity check); its generation is in progress and nothing has been scored (3.9.0). The Laya head was trained in 3.6.0 (Section~\ref{sec:laya}); still not done: a generator adapter and the 27B tier.
\end{itemize}
